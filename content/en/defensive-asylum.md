<h2 id="what-is-defensive-asylum">What is the difference between affirmative and defensive asylum?</h2>
<p>Defensive asylum is requested before an immigration judge when you are already in removal proceedings; affirmative asylum is filed with USCIS when you are not. Both use Form I-589 and generally the one-year deadline. In court, an ICE attorney represents the government and can question you (<a href="https://www.uscis.gov/humanitarian/refugees-and-asylum/asylum/obtaining-asylum-in-the-united-states">USCIS</a>).</p>

## How do affirmative and defensive asylum compare?

| | Affirmative asylum | Defensive asylum |
|---|---|---|
| When | You are not in removal proceedings (except unaccompanied children) | You are in removal proceedings in immigration court |
| Where Form I-589 is filed | With USCIS | With the immigration judge (EOIR) |
| Who decides | A USCIS asylum officer, in a non-adversarial interview | An immigration judge, in an adversarial hearing |
| The government | No government attorney | An ICE attorney argues the government's side |
| If not granted | USCIS may refer the case to a judge, who reviews it again | The judge considers other relief and, if none, orders removal; either side can appeal |

Source: [USCIS, Obtaining Asylum in the United States](https://www.uscis.gov/humanitarian/refugees-and-asylum/asylum/obtaining-asylum-in-the-united-states). If your case reached court after a positive credible fear interview and a Notice to Appear, you are also in the defensive process.

## Which protections does Form I-589 cover?

If you fear returning to your country, three protections exist. All are requested on **Form I-589**, and the judge considers them in order.

| | Asylum | Withholding | CAT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burden of proof | Well-founded fear | More likely than not | More likely than not to be tortured |
| One-year deadline | Yes | No | No |
| Protected ground required | Yes | Yes | No |
| Path to a green card | Yes, after one year | No | No |
| Can include family | Yes (spouse, children) | No | No |

## What must you prove to win defensive asylum?

To win asylum you must show past persecution or a well-founded fear of it, **on account of** one of five protected grounds: race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a **particular social group**. That last ground is the most contested and the most changeable — it determines whether domestic violence, gang violence and sexual orientation claims qualify, and the answer has swung between administrations and circuits. An attorney must analyze it under the case law currently binding in your circuit.

You must also show the persecutor is the government or someone it cannot or will not control, and that you could not simply relocate within your country.

### What is the one-year asylum deadline?

You generally must file within **one year** of your last arrival ([INA § 208(a)(2)(B)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title8/html/USCODE-2024-title8-chap12-subchapII-partI-sec1158.htm); [8 C.F.R. § 1208.4(a)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-V/subchapter-B/part-1208/subpart-A/section-1208.4)). Two exceptions exist: *changed circumstances* and *extraordinary circumstances*. If you miss the deadline without an exception, the judge will deny asylum but may still grant withholding or CAT.

### Who is barred from asylum?

Asylum is unavailable to anyone who persecuted others, was convicted of a particularly serious crime, poses a security danger, or can be resettled in a safe third country, among other bars.

## What is withholding of removal?

If asylum is unavailable — because of the deadline, a conviction, or a discretionary denial — **withholding** under INA § 241(b)(3) may still apply. The burden is higher: you must show it is **more likely than not** that you would face persecution on a protected ground.

It is narrower protection: no green card, no derivative status for family, no travel, and technically the removal order exists but is not executed to that country. It does allow work authorization.

## What does CAT protection cover?

The Convention Against Torture protects anyone who shows it is more likely than not that they would be **tortured** with the consent or acquiescence of a public official. No protected ground is required, and criminal bars do not apply — even someone with serious convictions can receive **deferral of removal**. It is the system's last safety net.

## How do you prepare an asylum case in court?

- **Your declaration**, detailed and consistent with everything said earlier — at the border, in a credible fear interview, on the I-589. Inconsistencies are the most common reason claims fail.
- **Personal evidence:** medical records, police reports, photos, messages, witness letters, identity documents.
- **Country evidence:** State Department reports, Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International, press coverage, sometimes an expert witness.
- **Certified translations** of every non-English document.

All of it must be filed by the judge's deadline, usually weeks before the individual hearing.

## What happens if an asylum application is false?

Filing an application the judge finds **knowingly false** can bar you permanently from any immigration benefit. Non-lawyers sell asylum "packages" built on invented stories. You are the one who signs the form, and you are the one who bears the consequences.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between affirmative and defensive asylum?

Affirmative asylum is filed with USCIS when you are not in removal proceedings. Defensive asylum is raised before the immigration judge inside proceedings. The form is the same, Form I-589.

### Can I work while my asylum case is pending?

You can apply for [work authorization](/en/work-permit-pending-asylum/) (Form I-765) once your asylum application has been pending for a required period. Those rules have changed several times; confirm the current waiting period with an attorney or on the USCIS site before filing.

### What if more than a year has passed since I entered?

You may still qualify through changed circumstances (such as conditions in your country) or extraordinary circumstances (serious illness, being an unaccompanied minor). Even if asylum is time-barred, withholding and CAT remain available.

### Where do I file Form I-589 if I am in removal proceedings?

With the immigration judge (EOIR), not USCIS. If you filed with USCIS first and were referred to court, the judge reviews that same application again, unless it was abandoned or dismissed.

### What happens if USCIS does not grant affirmative asylum?

If you lack lawful status, USCIS issues a Notice to Appear and refers the case to an immigration judge, and you move into the defensive process, where the judge reviews the application again.

### Can an unaccompanied child file asylum with USCIS while in proceedings?

Yes. According to USCIS, unaccompanied children are the exception: they may file asylum with USCIS even while in removal proceedings.
