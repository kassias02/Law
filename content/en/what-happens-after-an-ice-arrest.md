After an arrest, ICE records the person's information and decides whether to open a court case. If the arrest was warrantless, the regulation requires a decision within 48 hours on whether the person stays detained or is released on bond or recognizance, except in an emergency. A weekend delays steps such as paying a bond.

**Notice:** this page explains the law and procedures in general terms, using official sources, as of October 28, 2026. It is not legal advice: talk to a licensed immigration attorney about your case.

## What happens in the first hours after an ICE arrest?

The regulation describes what must happen after a warrantless arrest ([8 C.F.R. § 287.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-287/section-287.3)):

1. **Examination by another officer.** The person is examined by an officer other than the arresting officer, unless no other officer is readily available and waiting would cause unnecessary delay.
2. **A decision on the procedure.** The officer can refer the case to an immigration judge, order expedited removal where the law allows it, or take other appropriate action.
3. **Information for the person.** If formal proceedings are started, the person must be told the reasons for the arrest and of the right to be represented at no expense to the government. This does not apply to expedited removal.

In practice, this stage includes fingerprints and booking. The family does not always know where the person is during the first hours: see [how to find someone detained by ICE](/en/find-someone-detained-by-ice/).

## How quickly does ICE decide whether the person stays detained?

Under [8 C.F.R. § 287.3(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-287/section-287.3), **within 48 hours of the arrest** a determination is made:

- whether the person will be continued in custody or released on bond or recognizance; and
- whether a Notice to Appear and an arrest warrant will be issued.

There is an exception: "in the event of an emergency or other extraordinary circumstance," the decision can be made within "an additional reasonable period of time." This rule is in the section on **warrantless** arrests, and it does not apply if voluntary departure was granted. The text does not say whether the 48 hours include weekends; we do not interpret that here.

Note: these 48 hours are a deadline to **decide on custody**, not a limit on detention. If ICE keeps the person, they can stay detained for the length of their case, depending on the legal basis: see [how long ICE can detain someone](/en/how-long-can-ice-detain-someone/) and [who is not eligible for immigration bond](/en/mandatory-detention-no-bond/).

## What if the person was in a local jail with a detainer?

A detainer is a request from ICE asking a jail or prison to notify ICE before releasing the person and to hold them for a short additional period. Two official texts describe it, with one difference:

- **The regulation** ([8 C.F.R. § 287.7(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-287/section-287.7)) says the local agency shall maintain custody "for a period not to exceed 48 hours, **excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays**."
- **ICE's current web page** ([Immigration detainers](https://www.ice.gov/immigration-detainers)) says "up to 48 hours" beyond the time the person would otherwise have been released, without mentioning weekends. It adds that detainers "are only requests," and that if ICE does not assume custody after 48 hours, the local agency is required to release the person.

We cannot tell you which calculation applies in a given case; it also depends on each jail's policy. If someone believes they are being held beyond that period, the same ICE page explains how to raise it, and a lawyer should look at it.

## What changes if the arrest happens on a Friday or a weekend?

The law does not change, but several official services only operate on business days:

| Step | Official hours | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ICE DRIL line, 1-888-351-4024 | Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern, excluding holidays. Spanish-speaking operators available. | [ICE](https://www.ice.gov/contact/detention-information-line) |
| Paying a bond to ICE (CeBONDS) | Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. in the time zone where the person is detained, excluding holidays. Later requests are processed the next business day. | [ICE](https://www.ice.gov/detain/detention-management/bonds) |
| EOIR automated line, 1-800-898-7180 | 24 hours, 7 days. Representatives during regular business hours. | [EOIR](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/customer-service-initiatives) |
| ICE detainee locator | Online, always available. | [ICE](https://locator.ice.gov/) |

What that means in practice:

- **A bond cannot be paid over the weekend**, even if ICE has set one. According to ICE, verification takes about 1 to 2 hours within posting hours.
- **EOIR's automated line may show nothing yet:** it only has information once the case is registered with the court.
- **A bond hearing before the judge** can be requested in writing or orally, with no fee; the court schedules it "for the earliest possible date," according to [EOIR's manual](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/policy-manual-eoir/part-II/icpm/chapter-8-3). We have not found an official source on court operations over weekends, so we do not give a timeframe.

## What should the family do between the arrest and Monday?

A checklist:

1. **Search for the person** in the [ICE locator](https://locator.ice.gov/) with the A-Number and country of birth, or the exact name and country of birth. If they do not appear, follow the steps in our guide to [finding someone detained by ICE](/en/find-someone-detained-by-ice/).
2. **Write everything down:** A-Number, detention facility, time and place of arrest, officers' names or badge numbers if you have them.
3. **Keep your phone charged and topped up** and answer unknown numbers: detained people usually call out.
4. **Gather evidence of ties:** proof of address and work, children's birth certificates, tax returns, letters of support. These help at a possible bond hearing.
5. **Get payment ready in case there is a bond.** According to ICE, the payer must be a U.S. citizen or permanent resident (or a law firm or nonprofit) with an accepted ID, and CeBONDS payments are made by Fedwire or ACH transfer. See [how immigration bond works](/en/immigration-bond/).
6. **Look for a lawyer:** EOIR publishes a [list of free legal service providers](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/list-pro-bono-legal-service-providers) by state.
7. **First thing Monday:** call the DRIL line (1-888-351-4024) or the [ERO field office](https://www.ice.gov/contact/field-offices) for the detention facility, and ask whether ICE set a bond and which provision it says the person is held under.

If nothing has happened yet and you want to prepare, see [what to do if ICE comes to your door](/en/what-to-do-if-ice-comes/).

## What should you avoid after the arrest?

- **Do not sign documents the person does not understand.** They have the right to be told the reasons for the arrest and to seek legal representation at no expense to the government ([8 C.F.R. § 287.3(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-287/section-287.3)).
- **Do not pay middlemen** who promise to "get the person out" without a clear contract. ICE bonds are paid through the official channels ICE describes.
- **Do not assume bond will be available.** The statute allows bond under § 236(a) ([8 U.S.C. § 1226](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title8/html/USCODE-2024-title8-chap12-subchapII-partIV-sec1226.htm)), but mandatory detention applies in several situations, and its scope has been in litigation since 2025. The case will continue in [immigration court](/en/immigration-court/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens after ICE arrests someone?

ICE takes fingerprints and information, reviews the case and decides whether to start immigration court proceedings, use expedited removal or take other action. After a warrantless arrest, the regulation gives 48 hours to decide whether the person stays detained or is released on bond or recognizance, except in an emergency.

### Can ICE hold someone longer than 48 hours?

Yes. The 48 hours in the regulation are a deadline to decide on custody after a warrantless arrest, not a limit on detention. Afterwards, the person can stay detained for the length of their case, depending on the legal basis for detention.

### How long can a jail hold someone on an ICE detainer?

The regulation says the local agency should not hold the person more than 48 hours, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and holidays. ICE's current web page says "up to 48 hours" without mentioning weekends, and says detainers are only requests.

### Can you pay an ICE bond on a Saturday or Sunday?

According to ICE, no. Bond payments are processed Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. in the time zone where the person is detained, excluding holidays. Requests received after hours are processed the next business day.

### What number do you call to ask about a detained person?

ICE's DRIL line, 1-888-351-4024. According to ICE, live operators answer Monday to Friday (excluding holidays) from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern Time, with Spanish-language help. You can also call the ERO field office or the detention facility.

### What should the family do over the weekend?

Search for the person in the ICE locator and write down the A-Number and facility. You can also gather evidence of ties and the documents of whoever would pay the bond, get the bank transfer ready, and contact a lawyer or a free legal service provider.
